## Codex's review (verbatim)

Lemyte’s biggest problem is that its marketing promises a calmer, smarter assessment experience than the product currently delivers. Desktop home explains the benefit; the supplied mobile home and GATE captures barely communicate a proposition at all. Inside the product, fragile error recovery, a desktop-shaped exam interface and unjustified “Passed”/“Topper” labels undermine the reason to pay rather than use free PDFs. The earlier review improved several details, but its new demo upsell interrupts feedback, and its sample-report CTA promises specificity it does not deliver. A sceptical student has reasons to investigate; the current experience gives them insufficient reason to trust it with money and three hours of work. Research connects presentation with perceived credibility, but does **not** establish Lemyte’s conversion rate: [Fogg et al., 2003, *How Do Users Evaluate the Credibility of Web Sites?*, DUX](https://credibility.stanford.edu/publications.html).

*Basis: supplied screenshots, current source and previous review. No completed live payment or authenticated attempt. Blank captures may reflect animation/hydration timing; they do not prove permanent failure. Rankings are impact hypotheses, not measured losses. S/M/L means local, component/flow, or substantial product work.*

The design system is internally contradictory: “motion … never decorates” coexists with constellations, magnetic CTAs and word-by-word reveals. Delete the compulsory spectacle. [Mayer & Moreno, 2003, *Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia Learning*](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/9_ways_to_reduce_CL.pdf) supports removing irrelevant processing in instruction; applying it to marketing is an extrapolation. Its public-page axe result also says nothing about the exam’s practical phone usability.

On the subject phone page, topic names consume nearly the entire visible table; even “Average” is clipped. Show topic plus average immediately, with annual detail expandable. This follows Nielsen’s recognition principle. The paper and GATE 2027 pages otherwise demonstrate a workable text hierarchy; preserve it. “Take **a EE** paper” needs a basic copy edit. “Unlocks everything” violates the owner’s own writing rules; neither phrase proves AI authorship.

**What is working — keep it**

- **“Practice tests that show you what to study next”** explains the paid advantage over PDFs. Preserve it and make the product fulfil it—Pirolli & Card (1999), information scent.
- **The shortened, account-free demo and preserved plan selection** remove unnecessary steps—Nielsen (1994), user control.
- **Official-paper examples, worked reasoning and paper-page jump links** make quality inspectable—Fogg et al. (2003), credibility; Hattie & Timperley (2007), explanatory feedback.
- **Upfront totals, explicit expiry and “Plans don’t renew”** make the transaction more predictable—Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1986), fairness.
- **Real founder identity and restrained visual branding** are sufficient. No invented success stories are needed—Fogg et al. (2003), credibility.

**Where the research is weak or contested**

- These studies do not prove how Indian GATE buyers will behave. First-impression research measures visual judgments, not purchases; learning studies do not validate Lemyte’s implementation.
- Four paid options are not automatically “choice overload.” [Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd, 2010, *Can There Ever Be Too Many Options?*, JCR](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235) found substantial variability, not a universal penalty.
- ₹999 and ₹1,099 do **not** establish a classic decoy: price and duration trade off. [Huber, Payne & Puto, 1982, *Adding Asymmetrically Dominated Alternatives*, JCR](https://people.duke.edu/~jch8/bio/Papers/Huber%20Payne%20Puto%201982%20JoCR.pdf) concerns asymmetric dominance, not merely neighbouring prices.
- Ranking and streaks can motivate some students. Ames supports caution about normative emphasis, not a blanket claim that competition harms everyone.
- Delayed retrieval is well supported; a universal retry interval and universally superior immediate feedback are not.

**Do NOT do**

- **Add fake scarcity, crossed-out invented prices or a deliberately inferior plan.** Fairness and decoy research do not justify deception.
- **Replace diagnostic feedback with shame, “Topper” promises or motivational slogans.** Use task-specific corrections—Hattie & Timperley (2007).
- **Remove exam difficulty to improve demo completion.** Preserve meaningful retrieval; remove interface obstacles—Roediger & Karpicke (2006).
- **Add more animated reassurance while errors remain unrecoverable.** Fix task continuity first—Nielsen (1994).
- **Claim scientific proof of conversion uplift from this review.** Test the proposed changes with actual phone users, completed attempts, successful paid access and subsequent practice.
